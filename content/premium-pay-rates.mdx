Premium pay rules (overtime, nights, weekends, holidays) that restaurant managers configure once and that flow automatically from **scheduling to timecards to payroll**, replacing manual adjustments at the end of each pay cycle. ## Context

Hospitality employees aren't always paid a single rate. Overtime, nights, weekends and holidays can all trigger different rates, and multiple conditions can apply to the same shift.

Nory needed to calculate these premiums across **scheduling, timecards and payroll**, replacing a process that still relied heavily on manual adjustments.

## 1. Discovery

I started by reviewing how **workforce management platforms** handled premium pay, mapping the different conditions and rule structures they supported.

I then spoke with several Nory customers across **Finance, Operations and restaurant management** to understand how these policies worked in practice, from defining company-wide pay policies to managing exceptions at individual restaurants.

The research helped us identify the core requirements:

- **When it applies:** specific days of the week, all day or within a time range
- **Hours worked:** premiums triggered after an employee works beyond a defined threshold
- **How they're paid:** percentage uplift or fixed amount
- **Who receives it:** hourly, daily or salaried employees
- **Which roles qualify:** rules could apply only to specific positions or departments
- **Combinations:** multiple conditions could apply to the same shift

For example, a restaurant might define a rule like **"FOH employees working Sunday between 18:00–22:00 receive an additional 25%."**

This gave us the core design challenge: **how might we let managers combine multiple pay conditions while making the resulting rule easy to understand and verify?**

## 2. Mapping the experience

Mapping the end-to-end journey revealed that the problem extended beyond rule configuration.

Previously, managers reviewed worked hours and **manually tracked premiums and bonuses**, which were then passed to our payroll team for processing. Premium Pay allowed these rules to flow automatically from **scheduling → timecards → payroll**.

## 3. Exploring UI options

I narrowed the exploration to two approaches: a **structured form with visible options** and a more **compact configuration using dropdowns**. Both supported the same rules, with different trade-offs between visibility and compactness.

I prioritised **clarity and verification over configuration speed**. Important choices remained visible, conditions were grouped around **when, how much and who**, and additional complexity appeared only when relevant. A plain-language summary let managers verify the resulting rule before saving.

### From rules to payroll

Once configured, premium rules were automatically applied to qualifying worked hours. Timecards showed the resulting **shift type, pay rate and gross wage**, giving managers and payroll a clear breakdown to review before processing pay.

## 5. Outcome

Premium Pay Rates was gradually rolled out as a **beta, with customers onboarded by our Customer Success team**. During the pilot, scheduled labour cost accuracy increased from **85% to 98%**, giving managers a much closer estimate of what they would ultimately pay when payroll was processed.

Our internal payroll specialist also reported a **significant reduction in manual work**. Premiums and bonuses that previously had to be calculated and added manually at the end of each payroll cycle were now automatically reflected in employees' pay.

*I left Nory while the wider rollout was still underway, so I wasn't able to measure longer-term adoption or engagement.*
